FIELD NOTES · NO. 04

Anatomy of a Tree

A cross-section guide to what's underneath the bark.

Cut any trunk straight across and you'll find the tree keeping a written record of itself. Every ring is a year; every layer has a job. The outer bark is armor. Just beneath it, a thin green cambium divides itself into new wood on one side and new bark on the other, the only part of the trunk that is technically alive.

Sapwood carries water upward from root to leaf, season after season, until it's eventually retired inward and sealed off. What's left behind at the center no longer moves anything at all. It has gone quiet, dense, and dark — a structural memory the tree keeps, not for growing, but simply for standing.

Specimen: Quercus robur, English oak. Cross-section taken at breast height, sixty-one growth rings visible.
